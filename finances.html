      Reserve fund health

      The reserve fund pays for major shared-property replacements (ponds, the paved trail, mailboxes, fencing, entry features). It's intended to help the Association fund those replacements without relying on loans or special assessments. Annual dues stay flat at $400/unit in 2026; what's changing is how much of that same amount goes toward reserves versus day-to-day operating costs, now that self-management has freed up room in the budget.

          Two adjustments versus the 2025 Reserve Study, explained:

          • Fountain removed. A $500 fountain system appeared as a reserve component in both the working reserve workbook and the professional Reserve Study. The Association does not own it. It's excluded from every total and chart on this page.
          • Interest rate vs. inflation rate. The professional Reserve Study assumes a 3% annual inflation rate for future replacement costs, but only a 0.25% annual return on invested reserve funds. A rate that low means the reserve fund loses real purchasing power over time even while its dollar balance grows. The study itself calls 0.25% conservative rather than a current-market rate. In practice, the actual blended rate has been better than that: as of mid-2026 about $51,000 of the reserve fund sits in a 12-month CD earning roughly 3.4% APY, alongside a money-market account earning about 0.15% APY. The percent-funded chart above uses the Board's own workbook assumption of a 3% return, matching the inflation rate, which is why it reaches full funding faster than the professional study's own, more conservative number would project.
